Research

Research paper · B.Tech project report Draft complete
Benchmarking Machine Learning for Illicit Bitcoin Detection

Baseline reproduction, graph feature ablation, and temporal drift mitigation on the Elliptic dataset

With Saanvi Gupta · Supervised by Dr. Shailee Bhatia, VIPS-TC

A structured review of 23 papers across five methodological paradigms, paired with a seed-replicated evaluation on the full Elliptic benchmark (203,769 transactions, 49 timesteps) using a strict temporal split. Published results often rely on overall accuracy and random splits, so this work reports illicit-class precision, recall and F1 over time.

0.825
Random Forest illicit-class F1 (± 0.001), reproducing the Weber et al. benchmark.
+0.002
F1 gain from four graph centrality features. Every one ranks outside the top 100 of 169 features by Gini importance.
1.8%
Static-model recall after the timestep-43 drift. Expanding-window retraining lifts F1 from 0.032 to 0.164 ± 0.055.

Takeaway: graph-topology claims need controlled ablations against strong tabular baselines, and live AML models should be retrained on an expanding window and monitored for label drift.
